This case study describes the live experience and the design decisions visible in the current product. It does not claim formal usability research or measured business outcomes.

01 / Problem

The product challenge

Buying beef online asks customers to evaluate quality, farming practices, shipping, and price without seeing the ranch or product in person.

02 / Design intent

The experience goal

Reduce uncertainty through plain-language proof points, then give shoppers direct paths to cuts, pricing, ranch background, and frequently asked questions.

03 / Key decisions

How the interface supports the goal

  • A shipping banner answers a high-friction question immediately.
  • Benefit cards translate production practices into customer value.
  • Repeated ordering links keep the primary action available.
  • About, herd, and FAQ content support trust before purchase.

04 / Interaction & usability

Helping users move through the product

Navigation mirrors the customer decision process: understand the beef, compare pricing, verify the ranch, resolve questions, and order. High-contrast typography and repeated calls to action improve scanning.

05 / Tools & skills

What this work demonstrates

Responsive commerce design, brand systems, product education, conversion paths, information architecture, content hierarchy, and front-end implementation.

06 / What I’d improve next

Next iteration

  • Add product availability and fulfillment feedback near ordering actions.
  • Show package comparisons in a compact decision table.
  • Add customer proof near the first ordering decision.
  • Test whether first-time buyers find shipping details quickly.

Thinking beyond the current release

The experience can grow into account, inventory, order-status, and repeat-purchase flows while retaining the same straightforward ranch voice.

This case study describes the live experience and the design decisions visible in the current product. It does not claim formal usability research or measured business outcomes.

01 / Problem

The product challenge

AI automation can feel abstract and risky to a buyer who mainly wants fewer missed jobs and a reliable process for handling leads.

02 / Design intent

The experience goal

Lead with the cost of missed calls, present four understandable service modules, and show a short implementation process before asking for a demo.

03 / Key decisions

How the interface supports the goal

  • Outcome-led language keeps attention on the customer problem.
  • Service cards package scope and starting prices consistently.
  • A four-step process makes onboarding feel bounded and concrete.
  • Pricing, FAQ, contact, and login paths support different visitor needs.

04 / Interaction & usability

Helping users move through the product

The information sequence moves from problem to solution, proof, process, and action. Repeated demo links keep conversion available without interrupting the explanation.

05 / Tools & skills

What this work demonstrates

SaaS marketing UX, content hierarchy, service packaging, conversion design, responsive layout, client-login affordances, and production implementation.

06 / What I’d improve next

Next iteration

  • Add case-study evidence tied to specific business types.
  • Clarify what happens after submitting a demo request.
  • Compare plans by use case as well as features.
  • Test pricing comprehension with non-technical business owners.

Thinking beyond the current release

The public funnel can connect to onboarding, configuration, reporting, and account-management screens to create one continuous customer journey.

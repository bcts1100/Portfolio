Restaurant / Menu / Online ordering

El Recreo

WEB-05

A mobile-first sample redesign for a Renton Mexican restaurant, with a 174-item menu, a clear split between pickup and delivery, and a direct handoff to the restaurant’s existing Menufy ordering.

Focus
Menu scale, pickup vs delivery, mobile ordering
Contribution
Web design and development
Status
Sample redesign / draft preview

The experience at a glance

Put a long menu and the order path on one small screen.

The preview opens with the Renton location, hours, and two actions: order online or read the menu. Pickup and delivery stay named, and ordering continues in Menufy rather than a second checkout.

This case study describes a draft pitch preview and the design decisions visible in that preview. It is not the business’s official site, and it does not claim the business hired this work or published it on their domain.

01 / Problem

The product challenge

A 174-item menu is hard to use on a phone if categories, hours, and the difference between pickup and delivery are buried. This preview is a pitch for that job. It does not replace the restaurant’s current site.

02 / Design intent

The experience goal

Help a visitor confirm this is the Renton restaurant, find a dish, and choose pickup or delivery without leaving the Menufy ordering the restaurant already uses.

03 / Key decisions

How the interface supports the goal

  • Home, Menu, Visit, and Order keep browsing and ordering as separate tasks.
  • The hero states Mexico City street food, Renton, carryout and delivery, and that the restaurant is closed Tuesdays.
  • Address, hours, and phone sit in one strip: 2801 NE Sunset Blvd, Ste A, up the hill from The Landing.
  • Order actions link into the existing Menufy flow instead of inventing a new cart.
  • The preview labels itself as a draft and not the official site.

04 / Interaction & usability

Helping users move through the product

Primary actions stay next to the hero. Location, a six-day schedule, the Tuesday closure, and the phone number are readable without opening the menu.

05 / Tools & skills

What this work demonstrates

Responsive web design, information architecture, large-menu structure, mobile ordering paths, content hierarchy, and front-end implementation.

06 / What I’d improve next

Next iteration

  • Test category jumping inside the long menu on a small phone.
  • Keep sold-out or seasonal items from looking orderable.
  • State the Menufy handoff before the visitor leaves the preview.
  • Confirm hours and the Tuesday closure with the restaurant before any public use.

Thinking beyond this preview

A later version could group the menu the way people order—tacos, platters, family meals—while keeping the same handoff to Menufy.

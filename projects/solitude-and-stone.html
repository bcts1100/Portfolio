This case study describes the live experience and the design decisions visible in the current product. It does not claim formal usability research or measured business outcomes.

01 / Problem

The product challenge

Photography sites must showcase imagery without hiding the practical tasks that turn interest into a session or help a client retrieve finished work.

02 / Design intent

The experience goal

Use the images as the primary evidence, then surface clear routes for browsing categories, requesting a session, and accessing a gallery.

03 / Key decisions

How the interface supports the goal

  • A concise hero establishes location, mood, and specialty.
  • Portfolio categories help visitors self-select the work they want to see.
  • Booking language sets expectations before the inquiry step.
  • Gallery access gives returning clients a direct task path.

04 / Interaction & usability

Helping users move through the product

The navigation separates discovery, conversion, and client access. Strong image contrast and short labels keep the experience usable even when photography dominates the screen.

05 / Tools & skills

What this work demonstrates

Responsive web design, visual hierarchy, image-led composition, information architecture, booking flows, gallery delivery, lead capture, and production implementation.

06 / What I’d improve next

Next iteration

  • Add short project stories to selected galleries.
  • Test inquiry completion on smaller phones.
  • Clarify loading and empty states for gallery lookup.
  • Track which portfolio categories drive the most inquiries.

Thinking beyond the current release

A mature version can connect inquiry source, booking status, gallery delivery, and follow-up while keeping the public experience quiet and image-first.
